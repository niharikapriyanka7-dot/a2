The timeless pieces

Seven classic garments that have earned their place in wardrobes for generations, with their history, what to look for and how to wear them today.

Early 20th century

The camel coat

Camel-coloured wool and camel-hair overcoats became popular in the early twentieth century, first as sporting and spectator wear before moving into everyday city dressing. The warm, neutral colour flatters almost everyone and works over both tailoring and knitwear.

Look for
Look for a wool or wool-blend cloth with a dense, smooth face, a collar that sits flat against the neck and shoulders that end at your own.
Wear it
Over a fine knit and dark trousers, or thrown over jeans and a white T-shirt at the weekend.
1920s–1950s

The leather jacket

Leather jackets began as practical wear for aviators and motorcyclists, then became a symbol of mid-century youth culture through film and music. Today a simple, well-fitting leather jacket is one of the most versatile layers you can own.

Look for
Soft but substantial leather, sturdy metal zips, neat even stitching and a fit that allows a knit underneath without bulk.
Wear it
With a white T-shirt and jeans, or over a dress or a fine roll-neck for contrast.
Late 19th century

The denim jacket

Denim workwear grew out of the needs of miners, farmers and railway workers. The trucker-style jacket became a classic in the mid-twentieth century and has stayed in wardrobes ever since because it ages so well.

Look for
Heavy, tightly woven cotton denim, strong bar-tacked stress points and metal buttons. It should feel a little stiff at first.
Wear it
Over a hoodie or striped top, or tied over the shoulders with a summer dress.
Workwear heritage

The chambray shirt

Lighter than denim and woven with a coloured warp and white weft, chambray was a staple of work shirts for generations. Its soft blue suits casual and smart outfits alike.

Look for
Even colour, sturdy buttons sewn on with a shank and a collar that holds its shape after washing.
Wear it
Under a navy blazer, tucked into chinos or worn open over a T-shirt.
Mid-20th century

The black turtleneck

Long associated with artists, writers and designers, the fine black roll-neck is shorthand for understated, thoughtful style. It sits neatly under jackets and coats and needs no accessories.

Look for
Fine-gauge merino or cotton that holds its shape, a neck that folds cleanly and cuffs that don’t stretch out.
Wear it
Alone with tailored trousers, under a camel coat or with a statement jacket.
18th–19th century

Leather lace-up shoes

Oxford and derby shoes developed in Britain and Europe and remain the foundation of smart footwear. Brown leather in particular bridges formal and casual dress.

Look for
Full-grain leather, a welted construction that allows resoling and a snug heel that doesn’t slip.
Wear it
With chinos and an oxford shirt, wool trousers or dark jeans.
Timeless

The grey wool coat

A grey wool coat is the quiet workhorse of a winter wardrobe. It works with black, navy, camel and colour, and looks equally right in the city or the country.

Look for
Dense wool with a good drape, a lining that’s neatly finished and room in the shoulders for a jacket underneath.
Wear it
Over a suit, a knit dress or jeans and boots.
