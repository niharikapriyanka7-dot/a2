    Buying classics

    How to spot quality

    • Check the fabric label for natural or high-quality fibres.
    • Look inside: neat, finished seams and generous allowances.
    • Test the buttons and zips: firm, smooth and securely attached.
    • Feel the weight: substantial cloth usually wears better.
    • Consider second-hand: vintage classics are often beautifully made.
    Wearing classics

    Keeping them current

    • Mix eras: pair a vintage piece with something modern.
    • Play with proportion: a relaxed trouser with a neat knit.
    • Edit accessories: one good watch, belt or scarf is enough.
    • Wear them often so they soften and become your own.

    Correspondence

    Questions about classic clothing

    What makes a piece of clothing a classic?

    A classic has a long history, a simple and functional design, and the ability to look right in many different decades and situations. Trench coats, oxford shirts, chinos, white T-shirts and leather shoes are good examples.

    Are classic clothes more expensive?

    Not necessarily. Many classics are widely available at every level. Because they stay in style and are worn often, well-made classics usually give more wear than trend-driven pieces.

    How do I make classic clothes look modern?

    Focus on fit and proportion, mix classic pieces with one contemporary item, and keep colours simple. A relaxed rather than stiff fit often makes classics feel current.

    Can classic clothes be repaired?

    Yes, and that’s part of their appeal. Good leather shoes can be resoled, coats relined and knitwear darned. See our care and repair guide for what’s worth fixing.

    Where should I start building a classic wardrobe?

    Start with the pieces you’d wear most: a good coat, quality shoes, well-fitting trousers or jeans and a few plain tops. Add others slowly as you find well-made versions.
